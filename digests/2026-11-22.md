# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-22

Six reads worth your time today. The theme of the day: **when small losses cascade**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. Why Watertight Doors Fail at 14% Capacity Loss: Inside Microsoft's Exchange Online PIR — Sahil (dev.to)
https://dev.to/sahil_5eec7e7c6004ad71a7b/why-watertight-doors-fail-at-14-capacity-loss-inside-microsofts-exchange-online-pir-4kmd

A forensic walkthrough of the Nov 12 Exchange Online outage: a latent lock-contention bug from a July update met a 14% capacity loss, health checks evicted healthy nodes, and the recovery itself became a thundering herd. A clear lesson in why bulkheads and graceful degradation matter more than raw capacity.

### 2. Post-mortem: Outage Caused by a Backlog of Delayed Jobs — Open Build Service Team
https://openbuildservice.org/2026/11/05/post-mortem/

Doubling background workers to clear a DDoS-driven job backlog OOM'd the backend and got the app host blocked by the database. A short, honest post-mortem on how the fix for a queue problem became the outage — scale the workers and you scale the load on everything downstream.

## Java / Spring Boot

### 3. Senior Java & Spring Boot Production Debugging: 25 Failures Every Backend Engineer Must Master — Architecture Unfiltered (Devrim)
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/senior-java-spring-boot-production-debugging-25-failures-every-backend-engineer-must-master-b5aa6988cfc2

A field guide to 25 production failure modes — HikariCP exhaustion, @Transactional self-invocation, JVM heap growth that isn't a leak, Kafka duplicates — each with diagnostics and recovery. Worth bookmarking as a runbook for the next 3am page.

### 4. Spring Boot in Production: Expert Level — Manuel Herrera
https://www.manu506.com/en/dev/articulos/spring-boot-in-production-expert-level

The traps that show up in the first week of production: proxies that swallow @Async and @Transactional calls, emails sent for payments that never happened, scheduled jobs running on three instances at once. Every claim is tested against a real Postgres with Spring Boot 4.1.2, before-and-after code included.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 5. How I cut our cloud bill by 82% in four weeks, and what it cost us — Byteria Technical Lead
https://medium.com/@scnozdemir/how-i-cut-our-cloud-bill-by-82-in-four-weeks-and-what-it-cost-us-9c4a22e1ea2d

From $1,472 to $267 a month in 75 pull requests: measuring real usage instead of requests, killing staging, and shrinking the database. Unusually honest about the trade-offs — production now runs on a single node and there's no staging environment — so you can judge which cuts are worth copying.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 6. RAG Pipelines for Platform Teams: Vector Stores, Chunking, and Evaluation — Yuval Glasman (Platform Monkey)
https://platform-monkey.com/guides/rag-pipeline-platform-engineering

A late-2026 reference architecture for production RAG: chunking as the decision that dominates retrieval quality, the current embedding-model field, hybrid retrieval with Qdrant's native BM25, and an eval harness wired into CI — with honest cost math throughout. The one to read before your team starts its next RAG pipeline.
