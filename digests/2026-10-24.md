# Engineering Digest — 2026-10-24

Six reads worth your time today. The theme of the day: **when healthy systems can't recover**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. AWS EC2 Launch Incident (September 21, 2026): Static Stability and Recovery — Ismail Kovvuru
https://medium.com/@ismailkovvuru/aws-ec2-launch-incident-september-21-2026-static-stability-and-recovery-848a972fe2d8

A system can look healthy while having lost the ability to replace failed capacity. This case study of the us-east-1 launch failures turns that distinction into concrete checks for control-plane dependencies, capacity planning, and recovery design.

## Java/Spring Boot

### 2. Our Staff Engineer Approved a Redis Cache. Six Weeks Later, We Exposed Customer Data. — Engineering Playbook
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-staff-engineer-approved-a-redis-cache-six-weeks-later-we-exposed-customer-data-8420cc513593

A fast cache annotation silently bypassed the tenant isolation the database query enforced. A sharp reminder that every optimization creates a new execution path, and security guarantees must be re-tested on that path, not assumed.

### 3. Spring Boot Beyond the Annotations: Internals and Production Engineering — Rajat Tyagi
https://iamrajattyagi.medium.com/spring-boot-beyond-the-annotations-internals-and-production-engineering-1a7099106482

Seven years of production Spring Boot distilled into what the framework actually does under @Transactional, @Async, and auto-configuration. Best for engineers who have been bitten by a proxy or a pool setting and want the mental model that prevents the next one.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 4. Overinflated Kubernetes Resource Requests: Cost and Fixes — PerfectScale
https://www.perfectscale.io/blog/overinflated-kubernetes-requests

JVM pods sized for their startup peak keep paying that price for their whole lifetime, and the scheduler bills on requests, not usage. The fix is to size startup and steady state separately, with a startup probe protecting the slow start.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 5. Standard RAG Should Be a Tool, Not the Architecture — LMX AI
https://blog.lmxai.com/posts/standard-rag-tool-not-architecture

One-shot retrieval is a component, not a strategy: route simple queries to one shot, ambiguous ones to clarification, and weak evidence to rewrite-and-retry, with a latency budget. Backed by benchmark numbers showing when agentic retrieval helps and when it hurts.

### 6. Building Production RAG: Retrieval Quality, Privacy, and Engineering Trade-offs — Jeevan Srivastava
https://medium.com/@thejeevan/building-production-rag-retrieval-quality-privacy-and-engineering-trade-offs-33bab4384dda

The right chunk was retrieved but ranked below less useful ones, so the answer was wrong anyway. Hybrid search with reciprocal rank fusion fixed the ranking, and the piece pairs that lesson with the harder production question: what happens when retrieved context is sensitive.
