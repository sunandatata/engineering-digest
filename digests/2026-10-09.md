# Engineering Digest — 2026-10-09

Six reads worth your time today. The theme of the day: **production reality** — what breaks at scale, what the demos don't tell you, and the patterns that survive contact with real traffic.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. Building Service Topology at Scale — Netflix Tech Blog
https://netflixtechblog.com/building-service-topology-at-scale-architecture-challenges-and-lessons-learned-f4b792f3f0d8?gi=69cf5516dcec&source=rss----2615bd06b42e---4

Netflix on the cascading bottlenecks of service-topology aggregation at extreme scale: immutable data structures creating GC pressure, power-law traffic defeating single-stage aggregation, and a three-stage pipeline with graduated redistribution. The standout lesson: be willing to break conventional wisdom when scale justifies it — but only on measurement, never speculation.

### 2. Engineering Lessons You Bought a Decade Ago — Why Are We Ignoring Them with Agents? — David Bechberger
https://medium.com/@bechbd/engineering-lessons-you-bought-a-decade-ago-7db3ea341d68

"Agentic AI is just a distributed system with a non-deterministic orchestrator." Maps a decade of hard-won microservices patterns — circuit breakers, bulkheads, dead letter queues, chaos engineering — onto agent architectures. Essential framing if you're building agentic systems and don't want to re-pay for lessons the industry already bought.

### 3. The Simplest Distributed Systems Pattern I Wish I Learned Earlier — Yaninyz Witty
https://medium.com/@yaninyzwitty/the-simplest-distributed-systems-pattern-i-wish-i-learned-earlier-df1294e14861

The idempotent receiver: design every handler so a duplicate message is harmless, and half of distributed-systems hardness collapses into a single question. Short, practical, and immediately applicable to Kafka consumers, webhooks, and retried RPCs.

### 4. Networking for System Design, Part 1: Your System Is a Network — Akhilesh Veerapareddy
https://medium.com/@akhileshveerapareddy/networking-for-system-design-part-1-your-system-is-a-network-525eafb2bd5d

Every call across a process boundary is a gamble with a latency tax and a failure probability. A clear-eyed tour of what the network actually costs your system — intra-DC round trips, cross-AZ hops, cross-region partitions — and why production outages rarely come from the obvious place.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 5. Building a Production-Ready RAG Pipeline on AWS — Lucas Siqueira Chagas
https://aws.plainenglish.io/building-a-production-ready-rag-pipeline-on-aws-lessons-learned-8b9e0a61df96?gi=f3989a43806a

The demo worked; production didn't — hallucinated citations, stale embeddings, retrieval timeouts under load. An honest walkthrough of rebuilding properly on AWS (S3/EventBridge ingestion, Bedrock embeddings, OpenSearch Serverless) and what "production-ready" actually has to mean for RAG: document updates, hundreds of concurrent users, and a vector store somebody owns.

## Java / Spring Boot

### 6. JVM Performance Tuning for Spring Boot: GC, Heap, and Profiling — Marcelo Domingues
https://medium.com/devdomain/jvm-performance-tuning-for-spring-boot-gc-heap-and-profiling-46e800360f0b

A no-nonsense GC decision guide: G1 as the right default, when ZGC's sub-millisecond pauses are worth the CPU cost, why `MaxGCPauseMillis` is a goal rather than a guarantee, and why generational ZGC on Java 21 changes the trade-off. Directly useful the next time latency spikes and someone says "just tune the GC."
