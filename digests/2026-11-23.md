# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-23

Seven reads on the failure modes that survive every test and green dashboard. The theme of the day: **when the safety net lies**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. Our Distributed Lock Worked Perfectly. Two Java Services Still Processed the Same Payment — Devrim, Engineering Under Pressure
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-distributed-lock-worked-perfectly-two-java-services-still-processed-the-same-payment-5e958f9d9483

A Redis lease expired while a paused worker held a payment, and a second pod took over — both executed. The fix isn't a better lock, it's fencing tokens so stale owners can't write. Essential reading if you trust distributed locks for money.

### 2. Redis Was 99.9% Available. We Still Sold $1.8 Million in Inventory We Didn't Have — Devrim, Systems at Scale
https://medium.com/@devrimcoder/redis-was-99-9-available-we-still-sold-1-8-million-in-inventory-we-didnt-have-81ff0c723297

The cache was fast, healthy, and confidently wrong: a stale worker overwrote fresh inventory after losing its lock, and checkout kept selling stock that didn't exist. A sharp lesson in why availability metrics say nothing about correctness.

### 3. Our Kafka Consumers Were Healthy. A Single Poison Message Took Down 43 Services — Devrim, Systems at Scale
https://medium.com/@devrimcoder/our-kafka-consumers-were-healthy-a-single-poison-message-took-down-43-services-8e063d4fd43b

One undeliverable event, retried without discrimination, starved a shared database and dragged 43 services down with it. The recovery started when the team stopped treating every exception as transient. Read this before you tune your next retry policy.

## Java / Spring Boot

### 4. I Reviewed 47 Spring Boot Production Failures. These 12 Mistakes Kept Appearing. — Devrim, Architecture Unfiltered
https://medium.com/@guvencanguven965/i-reviewed-47-spring-boot-production-failures-these-12-mistakes-kept-appearing-5029292aa75e

HikariCP exhaustion, @Transactional self-invocation, deadlocks, and K8s OOMKills — the same dozen traps across 47 incidents, each passing every test first. A practical checklist for anyone shipping Spring Boot services others depend on.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 5. RAG in Production: Retrieval Is Data Engineering, Not a Prompt Trick — André Claudino
https://medium.com/@andrelgcclaudino/rag-in-production-retrieval-is-data-engineering-not-a-prompt-trick-d07c3defe223

Your grounded answers can never beat your retrieval: measure Recall@K separately from generation, build a golden question set, and check grounding at runtime. The clearest framing of why most RAG failures are data-pipeline failures.

### 6. RAG Pipelines in Production: Indexing, Retrieval, and Evaluation — Kinda Technical
https://kindatechnical.com/mlops-guide/rag-pipelines-in-production-indexing-retrieval-and-evaluation.html

Stale indexes, deleted documents that keep answering, permissions that drifted from the source — this walks through incremental indexing, deterministic chunk IDs, and access control enforced inside search. The operational half of RAG that demos skip.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 7. Cost as a Quality Attribute: Architecting for Unit Economics — Kinda Technical
https://kindatechnical.com/software-architecture/cost-as-a-quality-attribute-architecting-for-unit-economics.html

A delivery platform's revenue grew 10x while its bill grew 14x — because nobody asked what each order cost to serve. Treats unit cost like latency or availability, with a worked containers-vs-serverless comparison. Good for any team whose bill grows faster than its usage.
