# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-18

Seven reads on the failures that stay invisible until they cost you: event history that lies, pools that punish, bills nobody can see, and RAG systems that learn to say they don't know. The theme of the day: **history, hidden costs, honest RAG**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. Your Database Is Correct. Your System's History Is Wrong — SYSTEM DESIGN | ProdRescue
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/your-database-is-correct-your-systems-history-is-wrong-7c3ea65914e6

A replay job rebuilt a reporting projection from valid events and produced a wrong balance, because delivery order was mistaken for business history. A sharp argument that reconciliation and correctable history are architecture capabilities, not afterthoughts — essential reading if you rebuild projections or recover from outages.

### 2. 25 Brutal Production Incidents That Expose Senior Engineers Who Only Know System Design — Systems at Scale
https://medium.com/codetodeploy/25-brutal-production-incidents-that-expose-senior-engineers-who-only-know-system-design-0eb710c8ace2

A catalog of failures where every dashboard stays green while the business data is wrong: double-charged payments, cross-tenant cache leaks, migrations silently overwriting newer records. Keep it as a design-review checklist — the recurring lesson is that a 200 response proves nothing about what actually happened.

## Java/Spring Boot

### 3. Your SaaS Is Getting Slower. Your Engineers Keep Blaming PostgreSQL — Devrim Ozcay
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/your-saas-is-getting-slower-your-engineers-keep-blaming-postgresql-b4743c38aa27

Dashboard pages that took under a second now take six or seven, and the database gets blamed because that's where the slowdown becomes visible. The real cause is usually upstream: transactions holding connections while waiting on unrelated work. A good walkthrough of which HikariCP and trace metrics actually separate a database problem from an application one.

### 4. I Asked a Senior Backend Engineer to Fix One Slow API. He Made It 10 Times Worse — Devrim Ozcay
https://devrimozcay.medium.com/i-asked-a-senior-backend-engineer-to-fix-one-slow-api-he-made-it-10-times-worse-ae1857e4c1a0

A payment API's p99 went from 180ms to 3.9 seconds while CPU sat at 35%, and the instinctive fix — a bigger connection pool — made everything worse. A constructed interview case study built on real failure mechanics: connection acquisition p99 of 2.6s, retry storms, and why tuning a component is not the same as understanding the system.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 5. You Can't Cut Cloud Costs You Can't See — Vikash Kumar
https://itsmevikash.medium.com/cloud-cost-optimization-see-fix-commit-repeat-95ea3d9c10c4

Flexera's 2026 report puts wasted cloud spend near 29%, and this piece turns that into a plain loop engineers can actually run: see where the money goes, fix the biggest leak, commit the change, repeat. Short and practical — a good starting point if your team owns the bill but nobody reads it.

### 6. Paying for Potential: A Practical Guide to AWS Compute Cost Optimization — Tamir Kafri Djerassi
https://medium.com/@teacher.tamir.k/paying-for-potential-a-practical-guide-to-aws-compute-cost-optimization-fc076f1357f1

With compute you pay for potential, not use — a workload at 5% CPU is a 95% premium for empty air. A FinOps analyst's hands-on guide to the usual traps: x86 inertia when Graviton offers up to 40% better price-performance, over-provisioning safety nets, and the burstable-credit tax, with the IaC and CLI fixes for each.

## Applied AI/RAG

### 7. Lessons From Building a RAG System That Admits When It Doesn't Know — Anirudh
https://medium.com/@anirudhece2020/lessons-from-building-a-rag-system-that-admits-when-it-doesnt-know-71930a117c2b

Built by hand without LangChain or LlamaIndex so every failure stayed traceable, this RAG system was measured rather than assumed: heading-aware chunking beat recursive splitting, reranking didn't earn its place, and a rank-aware guardrail fix took expected-status accuracy from 0.73 to 1.00 with citation validity at 1.00. The honest failures are kept in the report instead of hidden.
