# Engineering Digest — 2026-10-27

Seven reads on what breaks when the systems meant to protect you become the failure itself. The theme of the day: **when the safety net fails**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. When Managed Platforms Fall: Postmortem of the Railway Security Incident and the PaaS Rotation Trap — Arijit Roy
https://medium.com/@arijitroy0445/when-managed-platforms-fall-postmortem-of-the-railway-security-incident-and-the-paas-rotation-trap-74844769ab51

After Railway's breach exposed customer secrets, teams trying to rotate credentials hit a second wall: the platform had paused all builds and deploys. A sharp lesson in why your incident response must not depend on the same platform that just failed you.

### 2. How a Kubernetes Database Migration Caused a 502 Production Outage — Naveed Ahmed
https://dev.to/naveedkumbhar/how-a-kubernetes-database-migration-caused-a-502-production-outage-27co

A rolling update plus an incompatible schema change produced a 24-minute outage that rollback couldn't fix, thanks to AccessExclusiveLock contention and pool exhaustion. The core rule: design migrations for the transition between versions, not just the end state.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 3. CircleCI's Runners Were Ready. PostgreSQL Couldn't Dispatch Jobs. — buildopsy
https://dev.to/buildopsy/the-database-was-the-healthiest-part-of-circlecis-fleet-it-still-took-down-3500-jobs-2fi5

WAL saturation on the scheduling database blocked 3,500 job starts even though the execution fleet was fully healthy. A reminder that restoring service and restoring normal operation are different problems, and that the control plane deserves its own capacity planning.

## Java / Spring Boot

### 4. Spring Boot Thread Pool Exhaustion: Why Your API Times Out While CPU Usage Stays Low — The System Design Journal
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/spring-boot-thread-pool-exhaustion-why-your-api-times-out-while-cpu-usage-stays-low-8083dd0590c0

A service at 6,000 rps with CPU under 40% still timed out because Tomcat threads were parked on a slow downstream and executors were starved. Useful for anyone who still equates low CPU with a healthy service.

### 5. Our JVM Was Faster After the Upgrade. Production Latency Got 3 Times Worse. — ProdRescue
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-jvm-was-faster-after-the-upgrade-production-latency-got-3-times-worse-f28d90cc780d

Benchmarks improved 18% after a Java 21 upgrade, yet p99 jumped from 240ms to nearly 800ms. The fix started not with GC charts but with asking what threads were actually doing during those three seconds — a good antidote to benchmark-driven rollouts.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 6. Your RAG Demo Works Because You Already Know the Answers — Zaid
https://pub.towardsai.net/your-rag-demo-works-because-you-already-know-the-answers-b83138babd11?gi=cae01eadeb3f

Demos succeed because the builder writes questions shaped like the answers. Production users type two vague words. This piece maps the five places a correct answer gets lost in a RAG pipeline, and argues most teams only inspect the last one.

### 7. Beyond Vector Search: Engineering a Two-Stage Reranking Pipeline for Production RAG — Mausam Adhikari
https://blogs.mausamadhikari.com.np/2026/10/beyond-vector-search-engineering.html

A bi-encoder retrieving the top-5 from a growing blog kept surfacing loosely related posts and pushing the LLM toward hallucination. Adding a cross-encoder reranker plus adaptive context pruning recovered precision for a few extra milliseconds per query — a concrete, replicable pattern.
