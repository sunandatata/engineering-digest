# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-03

Seven reads worth your time today. The theme of the day: **make the invisible failure visible**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. The Dual-Write Dilemma: Engineering Reliable Event Delivery in Distributed AWS Architectures — AWS Builder
https://builder.aws.com/content/3LQDVpSimZohBSEHp5kibrwXcjK/the-dual-write-dilemma-engineering-reliable-event-delivery-in-distributed-aws-architectures

A database write succeeding does not mean downstream services ever hear about it. This piece walks through the transactional outbox, change data capture, and idempotency, and is honest about what the outbox still does not solve. For anyone building event-driven services on AWS.

### 2. Engineering Correctness in Distributed Systems — Alex Chang
https://medium.com/@herefindalex/engineering-correctness-in-distributed-systems-33e2d9950dfb

A timeout hides three different outcomes, and the safe retry depends on which one happened. Start from the invariants a workflow must never break, then choose consistency mechanisms and tests from there. A methodical read for engineers designing order, inventory, and payment flows.

## Java / Spring Boot

### 3. Spring Boot Beyond the Annotations: Internals and Production Engineering — Rajat Tyagi
https://iamrajattyagi.medium.com/spring-boot-beyond-the-annotations-internals-and-production-engineering-1a7099106482

Seven years of production Spring Boot distilled into what actually happens at startup, why proxies break transactions, and how rollouts drop requests. Worth reading before the next 2 a.m. page, especially the notes on Boot 4 and Framework 7 changes.

### 4. Passed Review, Failed in Prod: A 7-Part Series on the Spring Boot Bugs Nobody Catches — Md Jahid Hasan
https://medium.com/@jahid.csedu/passed-review-failed-in-prod-a-7-part-series-on-the-spring-boot-bugs-nobody-catches-c2f54e4c2286

Bugs that sail through review and only break against a real database, real traffic, or a double-click: transactions that do not roll back, lost updates, pagination that dies at page 10,000, and retries that charge twice. A good checklist to keep beside your next code review.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 5. Northflank Security Incident (October 30, 2026): What Happened and How to Protect Your Secrets — Qovery Blog
https://www.qovery.com/blog/northflank-security-incident-secrets-own-cloud

Northflank told users to assume environment variables were decrypted and rotate everything after its October 30 incident. The timeline and rotation order are the useful parts here, plus a broader lesson: a shared control plane holding everyone's secrets is a blast-radius multiplier.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 6. What Production RAG Needs That the Demo Doesn't — Prav (TechDelta)
https://techdelta.medium.com/what-production-rag-needs-that-the-demo-doesnt-32373e32d4df

From live phone-call retrieval: documents you do not control, one shared index across tenants, and failures that raise no error, just a confident wrong answer. The fixes are ordinary engineering discipline, tenant isolation, retrieval evals in CI, and keeping model calls out of the retrieval path.

### 7. Beyond Vector Search: Engineering a Two-Stage Reranking Pipeline for Production RAG — Mausam Adhikari
https://blogs.mausamadhikari.com.np/2026/10/beyond-vector-search-engineering.html

A bi-encoder alone pulled dozens of loosely related posts and the answers drifted. Adding a cross-encoder reranker and token-aware context construction cut the noise sharply while staying inside the context budget. A concrete, reproducible pattern for anyone whose RAG answers feel vaguely right.
