# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-13

The failures that cost the most are the quiet ones: a retry policy that becomes the outage, a thread pool that was secretly protecting the database, and an observability bill bigger than the infrastructure it watches. The theme of the day: **silent failures and hidden bills**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. 5 Production Mistakes Even 10+ Year Backend Engineers Still Make — Sopheaktra Yorn
https://medium.com/@yornsopheaktra/5-production-mistakes-even-10-year-backend-engineers-still-make-e52fe3039c83

A checklist of the traps that survive a decade of experience: network calls inside open transactions, retries without jitter, at-least-once treated as exactly-once, OFFSET pagination at scale, and microservices as an org-chart fix. Worth reading before your next 3 a.m. page, not after it.

### 2. Your SaaS Has 20 Microservices. Nobody Knows Which One Will Fail First — Software Engineering Review
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/your-saas-has-20-microservices-nobody-knows-which-one-will-fail-first-3712f7644064

Every service can report healthy while a business operation silently leaves inconsistent data behind. The fix is to trace the lifecycle of an operation — who owns the state, which transitions are allowed, how stuck operations get detected — instead of reviewing service boundaries in isolation.

## Java / Spring Boot

### 3. 12 Spring Boot Production Problems That Expose Engineers Who Only Know the Framework — SYSTEM DESIGN / ProdRescue
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/12-spring-boot-production-problems-that-expose-engineers-who-only-know-the-framework-76dbc55322fc

From HikariCP exhaustion to @Transactional quietly destroying throughput and the Hibernate N+1 that passes every unit test — a tour of the failure modes that only show up under real traffic, and the diagnostic habits that separate framework users from production engineers.

### 4. The Senior Engineer Who Made Our API 10 Times Faster by Deleting Code — Production War Stories
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/the-senior-engineer-who-made-our-api-10-times-faster-by-deleting-code-726a0c5504e3

Six months of thread-pool tuning, JVM flags, and scaling from 12 to 32 pods — then one engineer opened the query plan, found an N+1 hiding behind clean layered code, and cut p99 from 4.2 seconds to 410 ms by deleting 600 lines. A reminder to measure the query before scaling the fleet.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 5. RAG in Production: Retrieval Is Data Engineering, Not a Prompt Trick — André Claudino
https://medium.com/@andrelgcclaudino/rag-in-production-retrieval-is-data-engineering-not-a-prompt-trick-d07c3defe223

Your grounded answer can never beat the retrieval that fed it. Covers golden datasets (start with 50–200 examples), chunking and reranking as data-engineering decisions, retrieval evals, and a real case where scoped retrieval cut LLM costs by about 77%. For anyone whose RAG demo works and whose production system doesn't.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 6. AWS Data Transfer Cost Optimization for Elastic Kubernetes Service: Case Study — Apiko
https://vercel.apiko.com/blog/aws-data-transfer-cost-optimization-for-elastic-kubernetes-service-case-study/

A real EKS surprise: pods scheduled randomly across availability zones turned routine traffic into ~1.2 TB/day of cross-AZ data transfer, about $12 a day. Node affinity brought it to ~75 GB and $0.75 a day. A concrete lesson that placement decisions are billing decisions.

### 7. Our Staff Engineer Added OpenTelemetry Everywhere. The Monitoring Bill Hit $186,000 — SYSTEM DESIGN / ProdRescue
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-staff-engineer-added-opentelemetry-everywhere-the-monitoring-bill-hit-186-000-6a2ee237473b

Full tracing across 43 microservices pushed the observability bill from $24,000 to $186,000 a month — more than the $142,000 the application itself cost to run. A cautionary tale about sampling, cardinality, and treating observability as a cost center with its own budget, not a free side effect.
