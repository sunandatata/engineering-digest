# Engineering Digest — 2026-10-23

Outages from components that were each doing their job, a PaaS breach that froze credential rotation, and production lessons on virtual threads, connection pools, and RAG evidence pipelines. The theme of the day: **failure radius, rotation traps, RAG evidence**.

## Distributed systems

### 1. Ten Ways Small Failures Become Production Outages: Failure Radius in Distributed Systems — JIN
https://medium.com/jin-system-architect/ten-ways-small-failures-become-production-outages-failure-radius-in-distributed-systems-0ab70e3a2a05

A taxonomy of how healthy components conspire to kill a system: retries chasing success, health checks chasing hygiene, autoscalers chasing capacity. The thesis is sharp and actionable - reliability is not preventing failure, it's pre-deciding how far each failure is allowed to travel. Worth reading before your next reliability review.

## Cloud infrastructure

### 2. When Managed Platforms Fall: Postmortem of the Railway Security Incident and the PaaS Rotation Trap — Arijit Roy
https://medium.com/@arijitroy0445/when-managed-platforms-fall-postmortem-of-the-railway-security-incident-and-the-paas-rotation-trap-74844769ab51

A breach of a PaaS secret store collided with a global build freeze, leaving teams unable to rotate the very credentials that were exposed. Names the failure mode the 'rotation trap' and makes the case for secrets architecture that never locks you out during an incident. Timely and unsettling - review your own secret-store dependencies after reading.

## Distributed systems

### 3. CircleCI's Runners Were Ready. PostgreSQL Couldn't Dispatch Jobs. — Buildopsy (dev.to)
https://dev.to/buildopsy/the-database-was-the-healthiest-part-of-circlecis-fleet-it-still-took-down-3500-jobs-2fi5

CircleCI's runners were healthy while WAL saturation in a backing database blocked 3,500 job starts. The lesson that sticks: restoring service and restoring normal operation are two different problems, and the control plane that schedules work can be the real bottleneck. Concrete monitoring guidance for PostgreSQL WAL that is worth adopting directly.

## Java/Spring

### 4. Java Virtual Threads in Production: What Nobody Tells You — Naveen Metta
https://naveen-metta.medium.com/java-virtual-threads-in-production-what-nobody-tells-you-f237299d7ff2

One property (spring.threads.virtual.enabled=true) gave 3.4x throughput in load tests - then 40,000 virtual threads sat waiting on a 30-connection pool at 2 AM. The real point: the flag silently removes your most important backpressure mechanism. Read this before enabling virtual threads; budget for semaphore-guarded resource access.

### 5. The 3-Line Config Change That Took Down Production: A Spring Boot & HikariCP Outage Post-Mortem — Sopheaktra Yorn
https://medium.com/@yornsopheaktra/the-3-line-config-change-that-took-down-production-a-spring-boot-hikaricp-outage-post-mortem-703c557991df

A 3-line 'optimization' to HikariCP config turned a 35ms P99 into 30 seconds while CPU, memory, and the database all looked healthy. A vivid case study of how pool exhaustion masquerades as everything-but-the-pool. Good companion reading for anyone who treats connection-pool defaults as safe.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 6. RAG Evaluation in 2026: Stop Guessing, Start Measuring — CoderBlog
https://www.coderblog.in/posts/rag-evaluation-2026-stop-guessing-start-measuring.html

A crisp, opinionated 2026 eval checklist: 200-500 labeled questions, four core metrics, evals in CI with regression thresholds, 1% production sampling, 5% holdouts for high-stakes features. The memorable claim is that teams who iterate on their eval set as much as on the system ship the reliable products. Practical enough to implement this week.

### 7. From PDF to Evidence: Building a Production-Ready RAG Pipeline — Sagar (dev.to)
https://dev.to/sagar_cbbf462e61e84d8cc17/from-pdf-to-evidence-building-a-production-ready-rag-pipeline-5gn6

Frames RAG not as 'giving documents to an LLM' but as building a reliable evidence pipeline around one - extraction, layout preservation, chunking, hybrid retrieval, citations, validation, monitoring, human review. A solid end-to-end checklist for document-heavy production RAG, with the trust coming from verifiable evidence rather than the model.
