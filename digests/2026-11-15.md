# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-15

Today's picks: events that arrive but still lie, a scheduler that recovered too well, and a re-ranker that hurt every metric it touched. The theme of the day: **when the obvious fix makes it worse**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. We Built an Event-Driven Architecture. Then Nobody Could Explain Where the Data Went — Production War Stories
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/we-built-an-event-driven-architecture-then-nobody-could-explain-where-the-data-went-0158d7db1153

Forty-three Kafka topics, eleven services, and an order that existed in five versions of reality — every event delivered, every projection wrong. The failure wasn't messaging; it was teams holding incompatible meanings of the same business concept. Essential reading before adding another topic to an event-driven system.

### 2. Our Scheduler Recovered From a Two-Hour Outage. Then It Executed 4 Million Jobs at Once — Devrim Ozcay
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-scheduler-recovered-from-a-two-hour-outage-then-it-executed-4-million-jobs-at-once-98d4f69b48d0

A two-hour outage ended, the scheduler found 4M overdue jobs, and recovery became a platform-wide incident — rate limits, full connection pools, retries multiplying the load. Covers SKIP LOCKED, worker leases, fencing tokens, and the missed-execution policy most system-design diagrams skip. For anyone who owns a job scheduler.

### 3. Your Payment System Says Success. Your Finance Team Says $400,000 Is Missing — Software Engineering Knowledge
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/your-payment-system-says-success-your-finance-team-says-400-000-is-missing-7ef41b6ddf69

Dashboards green, 99.97% availability, and a $400K gap between the payment provider and the ledger: a timeout that retried with a new transaction ID charged a customer twice. The core lesson — a successful API response is not a finished business operation, and idempotency keys must be stable across retries.

## Java/Spring Boot

### 4. We Enabled Java 21 Virtual Threads. Our API Got Slower, and Nobody Could Explain Why — Software Engineering Review By Devrim
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/we-enabled-java-21-virtual-threads-our-api-got-slower-and-nobody-could-explain-why-273d11fa136c

One config flag, a benchmark that looked great, and a checkout endpoint that got slower in production: virtual threads removed the thread bottleneck and let the app overwhelm its connection pool instead. A clear reminder that virtual threads scale concurrency management, not downstream capacity — read before flipping spring.threads.virtual.enabled.

### 5. 12 Spring Boot Production Problems That Expose Engineers Who Only Know the Framework — SYSTEM DESIGN
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/12-spring-boot-production-problems-that-expose-engineers-who-only-know-the-framework-76dbc55322fc

A p99 jump from 80ms to 12 seconds with normal CPU, healthy memory, and a fine database — where do you start? Twelve production traps from HikariCP exhaustion to @Transactional throughput kills, framed as the diagnostic method that separates framework knowledge from production skill. A solid interview-prep and on-call reference.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 6. ECS or Kubernetes: How to Actually Choose — Ivan Tomica
https://www.sysctl.hr/blog/ecs-or-kubernetes-how-to-actually-choose/

The short version: on AWS, ECS is the default and Kubernetes is the exception you opt into for specific reasons — most teams under ~30 engineers carry ongoing EKS operational work for capabilities they rarely use. Includes the real cost math (including EKS extended support at 6x control-plane price) and a decision matrix that doesn't assume you're Google.

## Applied AI/RAG

### 7. I added a re-ranker to my RAG pipeline. It made everything worse — Muhammad Aqib
https://medium.com/@aqibbuzdar5/i-added-a-re-ranker-to-my-rag-pipeline-it-made-everything-worse-c2989296d7ed

Fifteen retrieval experiments later: the cross-encoder re-ranker lowered recall, lowered ranking quality, and made each request ~80x slower — removing it improved every metric (Recall@5 0.489 to 0.508, p95 latency 11.5s to 0.14s). A measured warning that a component's benchmark is not your benchmark; measure on your own data before adopting the checklist.
