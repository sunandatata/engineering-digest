# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-19

Seven reads on the failures that cascade and the numbers that cut through the hype: a health check that took down Outlook, a migration that waited on one lock, virtual threads measured rather than assumed, and RAG weighed against a million-token window. The theme of the day: **cascades, pools, and honest measurements**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. Why Outlook Stayed Down for 20 Hours — Srikanth Badavath
https://srikanthbadavath.com/blog/posts/outlook-outage-cascading-failure.html

On Nov 12, a faulty health-check component started failing healthy servers, the survivors overloaded and failed their checks too, and restarting everything at once re-triggered the collapse. A clear walkthrough of the feedback loop — and the boring fixes (gradual restarts, jittered retries) that would have capped it. For anyone who builds health checks and has never load-tested them.

### 2. Our Database Migration Took 200 Milliseconds. Production Was Down for 47 Minutes — Startup Founder — ProdRescue
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-database-migration-took-200-milliseconds-production-was-down-for-47-minutes-664a11855e33

Adding a nullable column took 200ms in staging and then waited on an ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock behind a long-running reporting transaction, queuing every write behind it. The lesson is that migration risk is measured in lock behavior, not statement time — and that a lock timeout would have turned a 47-minute outage into a failed deploy. Essential before your next schema change.

## Java/Spring Boot

### 3. Virtual threads in Spring Boot: I measured, here’s when they pay off (and when they don’t) — DJENADI AMIR REDOUANE
https://medium.com/@amirredouane/virtual-threads-in-spring-boot-i-measured-heres-when-they-pay-off-and-when-they-don-t-b605e96bdb9f

Same app, same machine, one property flipped, load-tested with k6: virtual threads gave 5x throughput on blocking I/O, zero gain on database calls, and a 5% loss on CPU-bound work — while peak memory rose by a third. The rare virtual-threads piece with real numbers instead of promises, plus the pinning and pool-sizing pitfalls to watch for.

### 4. Our Staff Engineer Scaled Kubernetes From 12 Pods to 200. Production Got 14 Times Slower — Staff Engineer — ProdRescue
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-staff-engineer-scaled-kubernetes-from-12-pods-to-200-production-got-14-times-slower-b4c46d919958

The autoscaler did its job and p99 went from 180ms to 2.5s: at 200 pods with a 30-connection HikariCP pool each, the Spring Boot fleet could open 6,000 connections against a PostgreSQL instance that could serve a fraction of that. The fix was scaling down. A useful reminder that replica count is not capacity, and that every scaling policy needs a downstream capacity model.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 5. Microsoft 365 Worldwide Outage Caused by Hidden Windows Bug — SOCFortress
https://socfortress.medium.com/microsoft-365-worldwide-outage-caused-by-hidden-windows-bug-91b6abb3bba3

A concurrency bug in an OS networking patch sat dormant on Microsoft's cloud gateway servers for three months, then took down Outlook, Teams, and Copilot worldwide for nearly 24 hours (Nov 12–13). The piece ties the incident IDs and timeline to concrete lessons on multi-tenant ingress dependencies and failover design. Worth reading alongside today's Outlook deep-dive — same outage, different lens.

### 6. Agents are sessions, not requests: size GKE node pools for AI agent workloads — Dillip Chowdary — TechBytes (source: Google Developers Blog)
https://www.techbytes.app/posts/agents-are-sessions-not-requests-size-gke-node-pools-for/

AI coding agents idle between prompts, so sizing GKE node pools like stateless web traffic wastes money: Google's Agent Substrate 1.0 parks idle sessions to object storage and cut node costs by up to 40% in their model, with a full pool hit only once in ~3,500 turns. A concrete cost model for a workload shape most platform teams are about to meet.

## Applied AI/RAG

### 7. Local RAG vs. 1M-Token Context Windows: Which Architecture Wins in Production? — Tech Reflector
https://techreflector25.blogspot.com/2026/11/local-rag-vs-long-context-models-2026.html

A million-token window is a capacity ceiling, not an accuracy guarantee: the piece weighs local RAG's selective retrieval, privacy, and governance against long context's cross-document reasoning, citing the lost-in-the-middle effect and NVIDIA's traced pipeline latencies. The conclusion is the useful part — most production systems end up combining both, and you should test evidence positioning on your own workload before choosing.
