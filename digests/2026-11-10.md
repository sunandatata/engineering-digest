# Engineering Digest — 2026-11-10

Seven reads on production failures where the obvious remedy backfired — and what to do instead. The theme of the day: **when the fix makes it worse**.

## Distributed Systems

### 1. Our API Latency Hit 18 Seconds. Adding More Kubernetes Pods Made the Outage Worse — Devrim Ozcay
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-api-latency-hit-18-seconds-adding-more-kubernetes-pods-made-the-outage-worse-0f16f0bd5c95

Checkout latency hit 18 seconds with healthy CPU and a responsive database, and scaling from 12 to 30 replicas made throughput fall further — the extra capacity mostly generated more retry work. A sharp lesson in why low CPU does not mean spare capacity, and why scaling an overloaded system scales the overload.

### 2. Our Redis Cache Hit Rate Was 98%. The Database Still Collapsed. — Devrim Ozcay
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/our-redis-cache-hit-rate-was-98-the-database-still-collapsed-865cfc1ed3a8

A 98% cache hit rate hid the real problem: one hot key expiring across instances at once sent thousands of identical expensive queries at PostgreSQL. Essential reading on cache stampedes, TTL jitter, and why the metric you celebrate can be the one that blinds you.

### 3. We Built an Event-Driven Architecture. Then Nobody Could Explain Where the Data Went — Production War Stories
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/we-built-an-event-driven-architecture-then-nobody-could-explain-where-the-data-went-0158d7db1153

Forty-three Kafka topics, eleven microservices, and an order that existed in five versions of reality — because a stale inventory event arrived after a newer one and overwrote the correct state. A cautionary tale that decoupling deployments is not the same as preserving business meaning.

## Java / Spring Boot

### 4. My Senior Engineer Added One @Transactional Annotation. We Charged 312 Customers Twice — ProdRescue
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/my-senior-engineer-added-one-transactional-annotation-we-charged-312-customers-twice-fefa320b31e7

The transaction committed perfectly and the client still retried after a timeout — because nothing defined what a duplicate business operation looked like. A crisp explanation of why @Transactional protects the database, not the HTTP request, and why idempotency keys belong in the design, not the incident review.

### 5. Spring Boot Production Troubleshooting: 15 Real Failure Scenarios and Fixes (2026) — Systems at Scale
https://medium.com/engineering-playbook/spring-boot-production-troubleshooting-15-real-failure-scenarios-and-fixes-2026-be3efb955b71

Fifteen failure patterns — HikariCP exhaustion with a quiet database, p99 jumping from 200ms to 8 seconds, Kafka lag caused by a stalled downstream — each with symptoms, diagnostic commands, and likely causes. A practical field guide for the on-call rotation.

## Cloud Infrastructure

### 6. Part 5: Halving the Azure Bill Without Losing Data — Emmanuel Mbagwu
https://medium.com/@lilstex4good/part-5-halving-the-azure-bill-without-losing-data-15ac50812a5b

A real Azure bill of $405/month for a small SaaS API was cut to about $205 with no data lost — the pricing calculator had missed half the costs, and AKS add-on reservations, not the app, were forcing a second node. Full of measured numbers and rules that transfer to any managed Kubernetes bill.

## Applied AI / RAG

### 7. Scaling RAG Systems: From Basic Vector Search to Production-Grade Retrieval — Victor Asel
https://medium.com/@victaasel/scaling-rag-systems-from-basic-vector-search-to-production-grade-retrieval-d57272eb782f

Getting a demo RAG app running is the easy part; production demands restartable ingestion pipelines, hybrid search with reranking, and balancing quality against latency and cost. A solid systems-engineering view of the jump from prototype to something you can trust.
